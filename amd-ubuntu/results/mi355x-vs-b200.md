## 1. RVS vs gpu-fryer (GEMM stress test, all 8 GPUs at once)

TFLOP/s per GPU. MI355X = RVS `gst` on ROCm 7.14, N = 8, mean of node6100 and node6101 (aggregate / 8). B200 = gpu-fryer mean of node5500–5502 (the 7 newer nodes are within 1%).

| Precision | MI355X (RVS) | MI355X on paper | MI355X % of paper | B200 (gpu-fryer) | B200 on paper | B200 % of paper | MI355X / B200 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| BF16 | 1,672 | 2,516 | 66% | 1,450 | 2,250 | 64% | **1.15x** |
| FP8 | 3,725 | 5,033 | 74% | 4,018 | 4,500 | 89% | **0.93x** |
| FP32 (MI355X) / TF32 (B200) | 154 | 157.3 | 98% | 751 | 1,100 | 68% | 0.20x *(not comparable: B200 uses TF32 tensor cores, MI355X runs true FP32)* |

On paper = vendor datasheet peak per GPU, dense (no sparsity), TFLOP/s.

**Apple-to-apple: no.**
- Different tools: RVS `gst` (hipBLASLt) vs gpu-fryer (cuBLASLt).
- RVS runs 30 s and reports the **peak** per-GPU sample. gpu-fryer runs 300 s and reports the **converged mean**. This favours RVS by a few percent.
- Matrix shapes are not the same: RVS uses 8192×8192×16384, gpu-fryer uses its own fixed shape.
- "FP32" means a different operation on each GPU (see the table). MI355X has no TF32.

FP4 rows: MI355X 1 GPU = RVS N = 1, mean of node6100 (4,079) and node6101 (3,990); 8 GPUs = per GPU. **On ROCm 7.14, FP4 needs one process per GPU:** with one RVS process for all 8 GPUs (the standard RVS run) the rate falls to ≈2,130 per GPU; a profile (2026-10-08) shows the FP4 kernel runs at full speed, but the GPUs sit idle 37–70% of the time because one process cannot launch work fast enough (`vs-amd-cloud/rvs-fp4-recheck.md`). With 8 separate one-GPU processes at once, each GPU gives 4,041–4,175 (32,784 total, test 2026-10-05), the same as 1 GPU. Real workloads run one process per GPU, so the last FP4 row is the one to use.

¹ B200 FP4 was measured only with all 8 GPUs at once (one process per GPU; 5,647–5,824 per GPU), so the 1-GPU row uses the same B200 value.

**Apple-to-apple: no.**
- cuBLASLt is **tuned**: it tries 28 shapes and up to 8 algorithms per shape and keeps the fastest. RVS runs one fixed shape with the library's default choice. This favours B200.
- The two B200 tools disagree on FP8: gpu-fryer gives 4,018 and cuBLASLt 2,524 (56% of datasheet). The FP8 ratio therefore depends on which B200 number is used (0.93x vs 1.48x).
- FP4 formats differ: MI355X uses MXFP4 (32-element blocks), B200 uses NVFP4 (16-element blocks).

**Results far from paper** (in the order of the table at the end of the file):

- **Why MI355X FP4 is far below paper (0.72x measured with one process per GPU and 0.71x on 1 GPU, vs 1.12x on paper):** MI355X reaches only 40–41% of its FP4 peak (B200: 63%). Its FP8 reaches 74% and BF16 66% in the same test. **The likely cause is the ROCm software: the FP4 GEMM kernels in ROCm's hipBLASLt library are less mature than its FP8/BF16 kernels, not the hardware.** **It is not power or clocks:** in the fp4 recheck (2026-10-08) the GPUs ran at 1.6–2.1 GHz and 260–800 W during FP4, well below MI355X's 1,400 W limit (`vs-amd-cloud/rvs-fp4-recheck.md`).
  - The test favours B200: cuBLASLt picks the best of many shapes and algorithms; RVS runs one fixed shape.
- **Why MI355X FP6 is only 12% of paper:** on paper FP6 runs at the FP4 rate, but RVS measures it at 30% of FP4 (1,240 vs ≈4,100). **The likely cause is the same as for FP4, the ROCm software: hipBLASLt's FP6 GEMM kernels are even less mature than its FP4 ones, not the hardware.** Evidence: fp6 and bf6 give the same number (not profiled).
- **Why B200 FP8 is only 56% of paper with cuBLASLt:** **all tensor-core precisions run at the ≈1,000 W power limit, and FP8 uses the most power per clock cycle, so it gets the lowest clock** (1,131 MHz, 58% of the 1,965 MHz maximum; FP4, BF16 and TF32 run at 1,300–1,360 MHz; `../cuBLASLt/summary.md` §3). Per clock, the FP8 kernel is efficient (≈97% of paper), so the low number is the clock, not a bad kernel. gpu-fryer reaches 89% on FP8, which at the same power would need ≈1,750 MHz. **The likely reason is gpu-fryer's test data, not a cuBLASLt problem:** gpu-fryer fills its FP8 matrices with random 32-bit integers, which are far above the FP8 maximum of 448, so every value is clamped to 448 (`gpu-fryer/src/main.rs`). Constant matrices flip almost no bits, use much less power, and let the GPU clock higher. cuBLASLt uses fully random FP8 data (the worst case for power), so its 56% is the lower bound and gpu-fryer's 89% the upper bound; real data lies between. A 1-GPU check is queued (`../cuBLASLt/job-fp8-check-1gpu.sh`): cuBLASLt with random, all-zero and all-448 data, and gpu-fryer, with clocks and power.

> [!NOTE]
> In my test, MI355X FP4 GEMM is only 0.72x of B200, against 1.12x on paper. The likely reason is that ROCm's FP4 libraries are not fully optimized yet, so a newer ROCm release is worth testing. In the other precisions MI355X is faster than B200, as expected; FP64 is 2.14x faster.

> **On paper, the B200 node is better than the MI355X node for GPU-to-GPU traffic inside one node: 900 vs 537.6 GB/s per GPU (1.67×), and any two B200 GPUs can use all of it, while two MI355X GPUs share one 76.8 GB/s link.**

busbw (GB/s) at the largest message size, **8 GPUs in one node on both systems** (one rank per GPU, the GPUs talk over XGMI / NVLink only). MI355X = RCCL on ROCm 7.14, mean of node6100 and node6101 (max 8 GB; alltoall 4 GB). B200 = mean of 10 nodes (max 16 GiB).

| all_reduce | 396.9 | 537.6 | 74% | 839.4 | 900 | 93% | **0.47x** |
| all_gather | 389.4 | 537.6 | 72% | 681.6 | 900 | 76% | **0.57x** |
| reduce_scatter | 388.7 | 537.6 | 72% | 691.6 | 900 | 77% | **0.56x** |
| broadcast | 389.4 | 537.6 | 72% | 682.1 | 900 | 76% | **0.57x** |
| reduce | 325.3 | 537.6 | 61% | 687.5 | 900 | 76% | **0.47x** |
| gather | 423.8 | 537.6 | 79% | 718.0 | 900 | 80% | **0.59x** |
| scatter | 395.9 | 537.6 | 74% | 731.2 | 900 | 81% | **0.54x** |
| alltoall | 347.6 | 537.6 | 65% | 658.1 | 900 | 73% | **0.53x** |
| sendrecv | 60.4 | 76.8 | 79% | 668.6 | 900 | 74% | **0.09x** |

On paper = vendor peak per GPU, one direction, GB/s:
- **MI355X**: 7 Infinity Fabric (XGMI) links × 76.8 GB/s = **537.6 GB/s** per GPU; **sendrecv** uses one GPU pair = one link = **76.8 GB/s**.
- **B200**: NVLink 5 through NVSwitch = **900 GB/s** per GPU, for every collective (a pair can use all of it, so also for sendrecv).
- **Note on all_reduce (expected):** B200 reaches 93% of its link peak, above its ≈76% on the other collectives, because **NVLink SHARP** (the NVSwitch adds the data up inside the switch) can improve all_reduce on B200 nodes; NCCL uses it by default when available (not checked in the B200 logs). MI355X has no switch and no such feature, so its 74% is normal for it, and the ratio (0.47x) falls a little below the 0.60x link ratio on paper.

**Apple-to-apple: close, not exact.**
- Same test design: one process driving 8 GPUs, 20 iterations after 5 warm-up, busbw at the largest size.
- Largest message differs (8 GB vs 16 GiB). Both curves are flat at that point, so the effect is small.
- The ratio reflects the hardware: MI355X connects the GPUs in a full mesh of XGMI links (537.6 GB/s per GPU in total, 76.8 GB/s per pair on paper). B200 connects all GPUs through NVSwitch (900 GB/s per GPU, any pair can use all of it). That is why sendrecv, which uses one pair of GPUs, is 0.09x.
- MI355X with 5–7 GPUs drops to ≈47 GB/s (see `ubuntu/rocm.md`); B200 has no such dip.

> [!NOTE]
> Inside one node, NVIDIA NVLink with NVSwitch is faster: AMD Infinity Fabric reaches only 0.47–0.59x of its collective bandwidth.

## 4. RCCL vs NCCL — two nodes, 16 GPUs (8 per node)

busbw (GB/s) at the largest message size (16 GiB; alltoall 4 GB on MI355X), **16 GPUs on both systems: 2 nodes × 8 GPUs, one rank per GPU**. Both clusters have 8 × 400G rails per node = 400 GB/s (specs in §4.1). MI355X = RCCL on ROCm 7.14, node6100 + node6101. B200 = mean of the 4 healthy new node pairs.

RCCL is AMD's version of NCCL and uses the same setting names, so the `NCCL_*` variables in this section (e.g. `NCCL_NET`, `NCCL_NCHANNELS_PER_NET_PEER`) are RCCL settings on MI355X.

| Collective | MI355X (RCCL) | MI355X ceiling | MI355X % of ceiling | B200 (NCCL) | B200 ceiling | B200 % of ceiling | MI355X / B200 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| all_reduce | 372.6 | 400 | 93% | $\color{red}{\textsf{233.1}}$ | 400 | $\color{red}{\textsf{58\\%}}$ | $\color{red}{\textbf{1.60x}}$ |
| all_gather | 372.3 | 400 | 93% | 375.4 | 400 | 94% | 0.99x |
| reduce_scatter | 371.6 | 400 | 93% | 382.4 | 400 | 96% | 0.97x |
| broadcast | 364.6 | 400 | 91% | 359.1 | 400 | 90% | 1.02x |
| alltoall, ROCm 7.14 | 77.1 | **93.75**¹ | 82% | $\color{red}{\textsf{48.9}}$ | **93.75**¹ | $\color{red}{\textsf{52\\%}}$ | $\color{red}{\textbf{1.58x}}$ |
| alltoall, ROCm 7.2.4⁴ | 89.6 | **93.75**¹ | 96% | $\color{red}{\textsf{48.9}}$ | **93.75**¹ | $\color{red}{\textsf{52\\%}}$ | $\color{red}{\textbf{1.83x}}$ |
| sendrecv³, ROCm 7.14 | $\color{red}{\textsf{39.9}}$ | **50**² | $\color{red}{\textsf{80\\%}}$ | 48.8 | **50**² | 98% | $\color{red}{\textbf{0.82x}}$ |
| sendrecv³, ROCm 7.2.4⁴ | 46.9 | **50**² | 94% | 48.8 | **50**² | 98% | 0.96x |

Ceiling = what the network can carry for that pattern, per node and direction:
- **400 GB/s** (8 rails × 50 GB/s) for the ring/tree collectives: the library runs up to 8 rings, one per rail, each crossing to the other node once, so together they can use the whole network of the node.
- ² **50 GB/s for sendrecv**: each GPU sends to one partner, and a GPU pair that crosses the nodes uses one rail (400 Gb/s = 50 GB/s). The slowest pair sets the busbw, so the ceiling is one rail, not 8.
- ³ MI355X sendrecv runs with `NCCL_NCHANNELS_PER_NET_PEER=4`. With RCCL's default it reaches only 32.2 GB/s (64%) on ROCm 7.14 and 28.6 GB/s (57%) on ROCm 7.2.4; how this was found is described below the table.
- ⁴ ROCm 7.2.4 = the host ROCm of our nodes, shown only for the two cases where it differs from ROCm 7.14 (see "ROCm 7.14 vs 7.2.4" below). All other rows: ROCm 7.14.
- ¹ **93.75 GB/s for alltoall**: there is no ring; each GPU sends half of its data straight to the other node and gets only its 1/8 share of the network (50 GB/s). In the busbw units the tests report, that is 50 × 2 × 15/16 = 93.75 GB/s.

Why only these two ceilings differ: busbw is defined so that for ring collectives it can reach the full network of the node (400 GB/s). sendrecv and alltoall don't use rings, so their ceiling is what one GPU can send over its own share of the network. Inside one node (§3) alltoall needs no special ceiling: each MI355X GPU sends over its own 7 links (B200: NVSwitch), so its ceiling is the same as for the other collectives; only sendrecv (one link) differs there.

**Apple-to-apple: nearly.** Same test, 16 ranks (one per GPU), same message range, same network speed. Differences: RoCEv2 (AMD Pollara) vs InfiniBand (NVIDIA), RCCL vs NCCL. reduce, gather and scatter were not run on MI355X across 2 nodes.
- all_gather, reduce_scatter and broadcast are equal: both fill the network.

**Results far from paper** (in the order of the table at the end of the file):

- **Why B200 all_reduce is only 58% of the network (MI355X / B200 1.60x vs 1.00x on paper):** RCCL reaches 93% while NCCL stays at 58%, though the other NCCL collectives reach 92–96% on the same network. **So the likely cause is NCCL's all_reduce algorithm, not the network** (which algorithm NCCL picked was not logged). **SHARP should improve it:** the InfiniBand switches add the data up, so each GPU sends it once instead of twice (2.2x on a reference cluster). It could not be tested here: the fabric has no SHARP Aggregation Manager (`sharp_am`) running, a switch-side setting the admins can enable (`../b200-nodes/sharp.md`).
- **Why B200 alltoall is only 52% of the network (MI355X / B200 1.58x vs 1.00x on paper):** MI355X (RCCL) reaches 82% of the 93.75 GB/s ceiling, B200 (NCCL) about half. Neither library uses a ring for alltoall: both send a separate piece directly to each of the 15 other GPUs (many point-to-point transfers at once). **NCCL's alltoall is known to be slow in this setup; the likely cause is the same as for MI355X sendrecv by default: too few channels per network peer for point-to-point traffic.** Not tested on B200; `NCCL_NCHANNELS_PER_NET_PEER=4` would be the first setting to try.

**How the MI355X sendrecv setting was found** (2026-10-08; `ubuntu/sendrecv-check.md`, ROCm 7.14 run in `logs/node6100/rocm7.14/rccl/sendrecv_714_20261008_201221/`). With RCCL's default settings, 2-node sendrecv reached only 32.2 GB/s, 64% of one rail, while B200 reached 98%:
1. **Driver and system setup: fine.** GPUDirect RDMA is on (RCCL log: `via NET/IB/<n>/GDRDMA` on all 8 rails; on AMD it needs no extra module like B200's `nv_peermem`), and the other collectives use the same GPU-to-NIC path at 91–93% of 400 GB/s, which rules out ACS.
2. **Network: fine.** Raw RDMA (`ib_write_bw`) reaches 98% of a rail with 2 or more queue pairs, on the same rail and across rails (one queue pair: 67–70%).
3. **The setting that helped:** more channels per network peer. With `NCCL_NCHANNELS_PER_NET_PEER=4` (or 8) sendrecv reaches **39.9 GB/s = 80% of one rail, 0.82x of B200** (default: 64%). By default RCCL uses too few channels for point-to-point traffic between nodes. On ROCm 7.2.4 the same setting reaches 46.9 GB/s = 94% (0.96x of B200).

**ROCm 7.14 vs 7.2.4 on our nodes (two nodes), and why MI355X sendrecv is only 80% of one rail on ROCm 7.14 (0.82x of B200 vs 1.00x on paper).** Compared: the same MI355X nodes and tests, only the ROCm version differs.

- **Ring collectives: the same.** all_reduce, all_gather, reduce_scatter and broadcast are 1–2% lower on 7.14, within run-to-run noise.
- **Point-to-point: slower on ROCm 7.14.** alltoall is 0.86x of 7.2.4 (77.1 vs 89.6 GB/s). sendrecv with `NCCL_NCHANNELS_PER_NET_PEER=4` is 0.85x (39.9 GB/s = 80% of one rail vs 46.9 GB/s = 94%). Default sendrecv is the exception: 1.12x (32.2 GB/s = 64% vs 28.6 GB/s = 57%).

**Cause: ROCm 7.14's RCCL uses a new network transport** (tested 2026-10-08, `ubuntu/sendrecv-check.md`):

1. **What changed:** ROCm 7.2.4 ships RCCL 2.27.7, which sends data between nodes with the standard `IB` transport. ROCm 7.14 ships RCCL 2.30.4, which uses a new `IB-CAST` transport by default.
2. **Effect:** the new transport is slower for point-to-point traffic (alltoall, sendrecv). The ring collectives are not affected.
3. **Proof:** switching ROCm 7.14 back to the old transport (`NCCL_NET=IB`) brings alltoall back to 89.6 GB/s (96%), the same as on 7.2.4.
4. **Best sendrecv:** the old transport plus 4 channels per network peer (`NCCL_NET=IB NCCL_NCHANNELS_PER_NET_PEER=4`) gives 49.4 GB/s (99% of one rail, 1.01x of B200). So the gap is RCCL 7.14's new transport, not the hardware or network.
5. **Caveat:** 4 channels per peer lowers alltoall by about 30%, so use it only for sendrecv-heavy jobs, not as a global setting.

The table above uses RCCL's defaults.

> [!NOTE]
> Between two nodes, AMD Pollara (Ethernet) performs the same as NVIDIA InfiniBand in most collectives, and MI355X is faster in alltoall and all_reduce. B200 all_reduce could improve with SHARP (data reduced inside the InfiniBand switch), which was not used here. NCCL's alltoall is known to be slow: it does not use a ring, but sends many point-to-point messages, and reaches only about half of the network here, while RCCL reaches 82%.

**Same on paper:** line rate per NIC and per node, NICs per GPU, PCIe Gen5. **Different:** Ethernet (RoCEv2) vs InfiniBand, and only the B200 network can reduce data inside the switch (SHARP). The UEC features of Pollara are not used in these runs.

## 6. Inference — Kimi-K3

**Compared: one MI355X node (8 × MI355X) running each of our three Kimi-K3 recipes vs two B200 nodes (16 × B200) running vLLM, with the same model, ISL/OSL 1K/1K and number of users.** MI355X serves more tokens per GPU than B200 with every recipe at every load. B200 streams one user's answer faster up to 64 users (lower TPOT). From 128 users up, MI355X has the same or lower TPOT.

**Memory: why 8 MI355X vs 16 B200.** Kimi-K3 (2.78 trillion parameters) needs **1,561 GB for the weights alone** (MXFP4 checkpoint), plus memory for the KV cache.
- **One MI355X node fits it:** 8 × 288 GB = 2,304 GB, i.e. ≈195 GB of weights per GPU and ≈90 GB per GPU left for the KV cache.
- **One B200 node does not:** 8 × 192 GB ≈ 1,538 GB, 23 GB short of the weights alone. B200 therefore needs two nodes (16 GPUs, TP8 × PP2: 97 GiB of weights + 59 GiB of KV cache per GPU; `../b200-kimi/results/kimi-k3-base-b200.md`).

**The setups:**
- **8 × MI355X (one node, TP8), three recipes:**
  - **ATOM:** AMD's ATOM engine ([github.com/ROCm/ATOM](https://github.com/ROCm/ATOM), not vLLM), same images as amd-cloud: [`rocm/atom-dev:nightly_202608111555`](https://hub.docker.com/r/rocm/atom-dev/tags) (`node6100/kimi-cloud/images.txt` lists the image digests); recipe: [ROCm/MAD Kimi-K3 benchmark](https://github.com/ROCm/MAD/blob/develop/benchmark/kimi_k3/README.md). `max-num-seqs` 64 up to 64 users, 256 at 128–256 users, 512 at 512 users. No speculative decoding.
  - **vLLM recipe 1:** AMD's earlier vLLM (ROCm) recipe ([recipes.vllm.ai/moonshotai/Kimi-K3?hardware=mi355x](https://recipes.vllm.ai/moonshotai/Kimi-K3?hardware=mi355x), 2026-09-25). DSpark speculative decoding up to 14 users, DCP 8 + CPU KV-cache offload above, server re-tuned for each load.
  - **vLLM recipe 2:** AMD's newer recipe ([amd-kimi-k3-recipe.pdf](amd-kimi-k3-recipe.pdf), 2026-10): stock `vllm/vllm-openai-rocm:v0.29.0`, one server for the sweep, `max-num-seqs` 128, no speculative decoding.
  - Detail: `ubuntu/kimi-recipe-old-vs-new.md`.
- **16 × B200 (two nodes, TP8 × PP2):** vLLM, `max-num-seqs` 64 up to 64 users, 256 at 128–256 users, 512 at 512 users (`../b200-kimi/results/kimi-k3-{base,improve}-b200.md`). No speculative decoding: DSpark does not work with pipeline parallelism, which B200 needs.
- **Both:** Kimi-K3 MXFP4, ISL/OSL 1K/1K, fp8 KV cache, prefix caching off, no expert parallelism, 10 × users prompts.

### 6.4 Apple-to-apple: partly

- **Same:** model and weights, ISL/OSL, users, KV-cache type, prefix caching off, no expert parallelism, median TPOT and TTFT.

- **Different by choice:** engine and version (ATOM or AMD's vLLM recipes vs B200's vLLM build), and `max-num-seqs` at some loads (vLLM recipe 2 uses 128 everywhere; vLLM recipe 1 is re-tuned per load).

## Quick summary: results far from what the specs on paper predict

On paper, MI355X / B200 is 1.12x for FP4/FP8/BF16 compute, 2.12x for FP64, 0.60x for GPU-to-GPU links in one node (537.6 vs 900 GB/s), 1.00x for the network between nodes and 1.00x for memory bandwidth.

**Results as expected from the specs on paper**, one per section (measured MI355X / B200 vs on paper; % of each GPU's own peak):

- **1. RVS vs gpu-fryer:** BF16 1.15x vs 1.12x on paper; both GPUs ≈65% of peak.
- **2. RVS vs cuBLASLt:** BF16 1.20x and FP16 1.19x vs 1.12x; FP64 2.14x vs 2.12x (both ≈97–98% of peak).
- **3. RCCL vs NCCL, one node:** Most collectives 0.53–0.59x vs 0.60x link bandwidth on paper; both GPUs 61–81% of their link peak. all_reduce and reduce 0.47x are also expected: B200 is helped by NVLink SHARP (in-switch reduction).
- **4. RCCL vs NCCL, two nodes:** all_gather, reduce_scatter, broadcast 0.97–1.02x vs 1.00x network; both 90–96% of 400 GB/s.
- **5. Training:** 7B models 1.12–1.19x vs 1.12x BF16; both use 43–46% of BF16 peak.
- **6. Kimi-K3:** Model fits in one MI355X node but needs two B200 nodes, as 288 vs 192 GB per GPU predicts; per-GPU throughput 1.05–2.43x across the three MI355X recipes.

**Results far from the specs on paper:**

| Result | Which GPU is off | Measured | On paper | Likely reason (one sentence) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **FP4 GEMM** (§2) | MI355X (low) | MI355X 40–41% of its peak (1 GPU and 8 GPUs), B200 63%; ratio **0.71–0.72x** | 1.12x | ROCm software: the FP4 kernels in ROCm's hipBLASLt are less mature than its FP8/BF16 kernels (74%/66% of peak), and cuBLASLt was tuned while RVS was not. |
| **FP6 GEMM** (§2) | MI355X (low) | MI355X 12% of its peak | — (not on B200) | Same ROCm software cause as FP4: the FP6 kernels are even less developed. |
| **FP8 GEMM on B200 with cuBLASLt** (§2) | B200 (low) | B200 56% of peak (gpu-fryer: 89%); ratio **1.48x** | 1.12x | Power limit: all precisions run at the ≈1,000 W cap, and FP8 uses the most power per cycle, so it gets the lowest clock (1,131 MHz); the kernel itself is ≈97% efficient per clock. gpu-fryer's 89% is likely inflated by its constant test data (every FP8 value clamped to 448); a check is queued. |
| **all_reduce across 2 nodes** (§4) | B200 (low) | MI355X 93%, B200 58% of 400 GB/s; ratio **1.60x** | 1.00x | Likely NCCL's all_reduce algorithm, not the network (other NCCL collectives reach 92–96%). SHARP should help but is not enabled on the fabric (no `sharp_am`). |
| **sendrecv across 2 nodes** (§4) | MI355X (low) | MI355X 80% (with `NCCL_NCHANNELS_PER_NET_PEER=4`; default 64%), B200 98% of one 50 GB/s link; ratio **0.82x** | 1.00x | RCCL uses too few channels per network peer by default; the setting lifts it from 64% to 80% on ROCm 7.14 (94% on ROCm 7.2.4; 99% on ROCm 7.14 with `NCCL_NET=IB`, so the rest is RCCL 7.14's new `IB-CAST` transport). |
| **alltoall across 2 nodes** (§4) | B200 (low) | MI355X 82%, B200 52% of the 93.75 GB/s alltoall ceiling; ratio **1.58x** | 1.00x | NCCL's alltoall (many point-to-point transfers, no ring) is known to be slow; likely too few channels per network peer, as for MI355X sendrecv (not tested on B200). |
| **Kimi-K3 single-user speed** (§6) | MI355X (slow) | B200 1.27–1.94x faster per user (TPOT) up to 64 users, depending on the MI355X recipe; same or slower from 128 users | 1.00x (same 8 TB/s memory bandwidth) | Not measured; most likely software (per-step kernel and launch overhead in vLLM on ROCm), since memory bandwidth on paper is the same. |

## Apple-to-apple?

**Short answer.** No table here is an exact apple-to-apple comparison. Each one runs the same kind of test, but the tools, matrix sizes, models or GPU counts differ. The closest matches are 2-node RCCL vs NCCL (same 400 GB/s network, same message sizes) and Kimi-K3 (same model and workload, but B200 needs 16 GPUs where MI355X needs 8).

| Comparison | Apple-to-apple? | Main differences |
|---|---|---|
| 1. RVS vs gpu-fryer | ❌ No | different tools; RVS reports the peak sample of a 30 s run, gpu-fryer the converged mean of a 300 s run; B200 "FP32" runs on tensor cores (TF32), MI355X FP32 is true FP32 |
