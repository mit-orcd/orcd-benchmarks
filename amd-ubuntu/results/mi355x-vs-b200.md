# MI355X (amd-ubuntu) vs NVIDIA B200

Written 2026-10-07. Every ratio is **MI355X / B200**: above 1 means MI355X is higher. For latency (TPOT) a ratio above 1 means MI355X is **slower**.

| | MI355X (this directory) | B200 |
|---|---|---|
| Nodes | node6100, node6101: 8 × MI355X (288 GB) each | node5500–5502, node5600–5802: 8 × B200 (192 GB) each |
| Network | 8 × 400G AMD Pollara RoCEv2 per node (400 GB/s) | 8 × 400G NDR InfiniBand per node (400 GB/s) |
| Software | host ROCm 7.2.4 for RVS (ROCm Validation Suite) and RCCL; apptainer containers (Primus, Megatron-LM, ATOM, vLLM) | CUDA 13.x, NGC containers, vLLM |
| Sources | `results/ubuntu/{rvs,rccl,rccl_2node,primus,megatron_ref,kimi}.md` | `../b200-nodes/out-gpu-fryer/`, `../cuBLASLt/summary.md`, `../b200-nodes/out-nccl-{1node,2node}/`, `../b200-nodes/output-megatron/`, `../b200-kimi/results/` |

**Short answer.** No table here is an exact apple-to-apple comparison. Each one runs the same kind of test, but the tools, matrix sizes, models or GPU counts differ. The closest matches are 2-node RCCL vs NCCL (same 400 GB/s network, same message sizes) and Kimi-K3 (same model, same serving settings, but B200 needs 16 GPUs where MI355X needs 8).

| Comparison | Apple-to-apple? | Main differences |
|---|---|---|
| 1. RVS vs gpu-fryer | ❌ No | different tools; RVS reports the peak sample of a 30 s run, gpu-fryer the converged mean of a 300 s run; B200 "FP32" runs on tensor cores (TF32), MI355X FP32 is true FP32 |
| 2. RVS vs cuBLASLt | ❌ No | RVS uses one fixed shape (8192×8192×16384); cuBLASLt picks the fastest of 28 shapes and up to 8 algorithms per shape (tuned in B200's favour); B200 TF32 vs MI355X FP32 |
| 3. RCCL vs NCCL, 1 node | ⚠️ Close | same test (rccl-tests / nccl-tests, 8 GPUs, 20 iterations, busbw at the largest size); max size 8 GB vs 16 GiB; different link design (XGMI mesh vs NVSwitch) |
| 4. RCCL vs NCCL, 2 nodes | ✅ Nearly | same test, 16 ranks, max 16 GiB, same 8 × 400G network; only the NIC type (RoCE vs IB) and library differ |
| 5. Primus vs Megatron (training) | ❌ No | different model (llama2-7B, seq 4096 vs GPT-7B, seq 2048), batch size and framework build |
| 6. Kimi-K3 inference | ⚠️ Partly | same model, ISL/OSL 1024/1024, `max-num-seqs` 64, fp8 KV cache, concurrency 1–64; but 8 GPUs (TP8) vs 16 GPUs (TP8 × PP2), ATOM vs vLLM |

## 1. RVS vs gpu-fryer (GEMM stress test, all 8 GPUs at once)

TFLOP/s per GPU. MI355X = RVS `gst`, N = 8, mean of node6100 and node6101 (aggregate / 8). B200 = gpu-fryer mean of node5500–5502 (the 7 newer nodes are within 1%).

| Precision | MI355X (RVS) | MI355X on paper | MI355X % of paper | B200 (gpu-fryer) | B200 on paper | B200 % of paper | MI355X / B200 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| BF16 | 1,672 | 2,516 | 66% | 1,450 | 2,250 | 64% | **1.15x** |
| FP8 | 3,748 | 5,033 | 74% | 4,018 | 4,500 | 89% | **0.93x** |
| FP32 (MI355X) / TF32 (B200) | 154 | 157.3 | 98% | 751 | 1,100 | 68% | 0.20x *(not comparable: B200 uses TF32 tensor cores, MI355X runs true FP32)* |

On paper = vendor datasheet peak per GPU, dense (no sparsity), TFLOP/s.

**Apple-to-apple: no.**
- Different tools: RVS `gst` (hipBLASLt) vs gpu-fryer (cuBLASLt).
- RVS runs 30 s and reports the **peak** per-GPU sample. gpu-fryer runs 300 s and reports the **converged mean**. This favours RVS by a few percent.
- Matrix shapes are not the same: RVS uses 8192×8192×16384, gpu-fryer uses its own fixed shape.
- "FP32" means a different operation on each GPU (see the table). MI355X has no TF32.

## 2. RVS vs cuBLASLt (GEMM throughput, all 8 GPUs at once)

TFLOP/s per GPU. MI355X = RVS `gst` N = 8 as above. B200 = `../cuBLASLt` **sustained** (60 s at the tuned shape), node5802-c1.

| Precision | MI355X (RVS) | MI355X on paper | MI355X % of paper | B200 (cuBLASLt) | B200 on paper | B200 % of paper | MI355X / B200, measured | MI355X / B200, on paper |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| FP4 | 3,195 | 10,066 | 32% | 5,708 | 9,000 | 63% | **0.56x** | 1.12x |
| FP4, ROCm 7.14, one process per GPU | ≈4,100 | 10,066 | 41% | 5,708 | 9,000 | 63% | **0.72x** | 1.12x |
| FP8 | 3,748 | 5,033 | 74% | 2,524 | 4,500 | 56% | **1.48x** | 1.12x |
| BF16 | 1,672 | 2,516 | 66% | 1,396 | 2,250 | 62% | **1.20x** | 1.12x |
| FP16 | 1,572 | 2,516 | 62% | 1,322 | 2,250 | 59% | **1.19x** | 1.12x |
| FP64 | 77 | 78.6 | 98% | 36 | 37 | 97% | **2.14x** | 2.12x |
| FP32 (MI355X) / TF32 (B200) | 154 | 157.3 | 98% | 720 | 1,100 | 65% | 0.21x *(not comparable)* | — |
| FP6 | 1,283 | 10,066 | 13% | — | — | — | — *(not run on B200)* | — |

On paper = vendor datasheet peak per GPU, dense (no sparsity), TFLOP/s. MI355X FP6 runs at the FP4 rate on paper.

**Apple-to-apple: no.**
- cuBLASLt is **tuned**: it tries 28 shapes and up to 8 algorithms per shape and keeps the fastest. RVS runs one fixed shape with the library's default choice. This favours B200.
- The two B200 tools disagree on FP8: gpu-fryer gives 4,018 and cuBLASLt 2,524 (56% of datasheet). The FP8 ratio therefore depends on which B200 number is used (0.93x vs 1.48x).
- FP4 formats differ: MI355X uses MXFP4 (32-element blocks), B200 uses NVFP4 (16-element blocks).
- MI355X FP4 on ROCm 7.14 is ≈28% faster than on 7.2.4 when each GPU has its own process (see `vs-amd-cloud/rvs.md`).

**Why MI355X FP4 is far below paper (0.56x measured vs 1.12x on paper):**
- MI355X reaches only 32% of its FP4 peak (B200: 63%). Its FP8 reaches 74% and BF16 66% in the same test. **The likely cause is the ROCm software: the FP4 GEMM kernels in ROCm's hipBLASLt library are less mature than its FP8/BF16 kernels, not the hardware.** Evidence: upgrading ROCm alone (7.2.4 → 7.14) lifts FP4 from 32% to ≈41% (≈4,100) on the same GPUs. (Power and clocks during FP4 were not checked.)
- The test favours B200: cuBLASLt picks the best of many shapes and algorithms; RVS runs one fixed shape.

## 3. RCCL vs NCCL — one node, 8 GPUs

busbw (GB/s) at the largest message size, **8 GPUs in one node on both systems** (one rank per GPU, the GPUs talk over XGMI / NVLink only). MI355X = mean of node6100 and node6101 (max 8 GB; alltoall 4 GB). B200 = mean of 10 nodes (max 16 GiB).

| Collective | GPUs (each system) | MI355X (RCCL) | MI355X on paper | MI355X % of paper | B200 (NCCL) | B200 on paper | B200 % of paper | MI355X / B200 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| all_reduce | 8 (1 node) | 393.8 | 537.6 | 73% | 839.4 | 900 | 93% | **0.47x** |
| all_gather | 8 (1 node) | 388.0 | 537.6 | 72% | 681.6 | 900 | 76% | **0.57x** |
| reduce_scatter | 8 (1 node) | 386.5 | 537.6 | 72% | 691.6 | 900 | 77% | **0.56x** |
| broadcast | 8 (1 node) | 386.9 | 537.6 | 72% | 682.1 | 900 | 76% | **0.57x** |
| reduce | 8 (1 node) | 328.3 | 537.6 | 61% | 687.5 | 900 | 76% | **0.48x** |
| gather | 8 (1 node) | 425.0 | 537.6 | 79% | 718.0 | 900 | 80% | **0.59x** |
| scatter | 8 (1 node) | 397.1 | 537.6 | 74% | 731.2 | 900 | 81% | **0.54x** |
| alltoall | 8 (1 node) | 342.9 | 537.6 | 64% | 658.1 | 900 | 73% | **0.52x** |
| sendrecv | 8 (1 node) | 60.5 | 76.8 | 79% | 668.6 | 900 | 74% | **0.09x** |

On paper = vendor peak per GPU, one direction, GB/s:
- **MI355X**: 7 Infinity Fabric (XGMI) links × 76.8 GB/s = **537.6 GB/s** per GPU; **sendrecv** uses one GPU pair = one link = **76.8 GB/s**.
- **B200**: NVLink 5 through NVSwitch = **900 GB/s** per GPU, for every collective (a pair can use all of it, so also for sendrecv).
- B200 all_reduce (93%) is probably helped by NVLink SHARP (reduction inside the NVSwitch, used by NCCL by default when available; not checked in the B200 logs). MI355X has no such feature.

**Apple-to-apple: close, not exact.**
- Same test design: one process driving 8 GPUs, 20 iterations after 5 warm-up, busbw at the largest size.
- Largest message differs (8 GB vs 16 GiB). Both curves are flat at that point, so the effect is small.
- The ratio reflects the hardware: MI355X connects the GPUs in a full mesh of XGMI links (537.6 GB/s per GPU in total, 76.8 GB/s per pair on paper). B200 connects all GPUs through NVSwitch (900 GB/s per GPU, any pair can use all of it). That is why sendrecv, which uses one pair of GPUs, is 0.09x.
- MI355X with 5–7 GPUs drops to ≈47 GB/s (see `ubuntu/rccl.md`); B200 has no such dip.

## 4. RCCL vs NCCL — two nodes, 16 GPUs (8 per node)

busbw (GB/s) at the largest message size (16 GiB; alltoall 4 GB on MI355X), **16 GPUs on both systems: 2 nodes × 8 GPUs, one rank per GPU**. Both clusters have 8 × 400G rails per node = 400 GB/s (specs in §4.1). B200 = mean of the 4 healthy new node pairs.

| Collective | GPUs (each system) | MI355X (RCCL) | B200 (NCCL) | MI355X / B200 | network ceiling GB/s | MI355X % of ceiling | B200 % of ceiling |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| all_reduce | 16 (2 nodes × 8) | 380.9 | 233.1 | **1.63x** | 400 | 95% | 58% |
| all_gather | 16 (2 nodes × 8) | 376.7 | 375.4 | 1.00x | 400 | 94% | 94% |
| reduce_scatter | 16 (2 nodes × 8) | 378.5 | 382.4 | 0.99x | 400 | 95% | 96% |
| broadcast | 16 (2 nodes × 8) | 369.9 | 359.1 | 1.03x | 400 | 92% | 90% |
| alltoall | 16 (2 nodes × 8) | 89.6 | 48.9 | **1.83x** | 400¹ | 22% | 12% |
| sendrecv | 16 (2 nodes × 8) | 28.6 | 48.8 | **0.59x** | **50**² | 57% | 98% |

Ceiling = what the network can carry for that pattern, per node and direction:
- **400 GB/s** (8 rails × 50 GB/s) for the ring/tree collectives, which spread their traffic over all 8 rails.
- ² **50 GB/s for sendrecv**: each GPU sends to one partner, and a GPU pair that crosses the nodes uses one rail (400 Gb/s = 50 GB/s). The slowest pair sets the busbw, so the ceiling is one rail, not 8.
- ¹ alltoall: 400 GB/s is an upper bound; only half of each GPU's partners are on the other node, so the exact ceiling depends on how the library splits the traffic.

**Apple-to-apple: nearly.** Same test, 16 ranks (one per GPU), same message range, same network speed. Differences: RoCEv2 (AMD Pollara) vs InfiniBand (NVIDIA), RCCL vs NCCL. reduce, gather and scatter were not run on MI355X across 2 nodes.
- all_gather, reduce_scatter and broadcast are equal: both fill the network.
- all_reduce: RCCL reaches 95% of the network while NCCL (ring) stays at 58%. This is an algorithm difference in the library, not a network difference.
- sendrecv: B200 reaches its one-rail ceiling (98%), MI355X only 57%, although `ib_write_bw` reaches 98% of line rate on every MI355X rail (`ubuntu/net.md`). The gap is in the RCCL point-to-point path over RoCE, not in the NIC.

### 4.1 Network on paper: AMD Pollara (Ethernet) vs NVIDIA InfiniBand

Vendor specs ("on paper"), plus what is set on these clusters. Measured numbers are in the table above and in `ubuntu/net.md`.

| | MI355X nodes (node6100/6101) | B200 nodes |
|---|---|---|
| NIC | AMD Pensando **Pollara 400** AI NIC (`ionic` driver) | NVIDIA **ConnectX-7** (MT4129, `mlx5` driver) |
| Network type | **Ethernet**. Pollara is sold as Ultra Ethernet (UEC 1.0) ready; **here it runs RoCEv2** (RDMA over Converged Ethernet), not the UEC transport | **InfiniBand NDR** |
| Speed per NIC | 400 Gb/s = 50 GB/s per direction | 400 Gb/s = 50 GB/s per direction |
| NICs per node | 8, one per GPU (rails 0–3 on CPU socket 0, 4–7 on socket 1) | 8, one per GPU |
| Per node | 3.2 Tb/s = 400 GB/s per direction | 3.2 Tb/s = 400 GB/s per direction |
| Host link | PCIe Gen5 x16 | PCIe Gen5 x16 |
| GPU-direct RDMA | yes | yes (GPUDirect RDMA) |
| Loss handling | Ethernet, lossy by default; UEC-style multipath (packet spraying), selective retransmit and programmable congestion control (P4-programmable NIC) | lossless link layer (credit-based flow control); adaptive routing in the switch |
| In-network reduction | none | SHARP v3 in the Quantum-2 switches (tested in `../b200-nodes/out-nccl-2node-sharp/`) |
| Switch | Ethernet switch (model not recorded) | NVIDIA Quantum-2 NDR |
| MTU | 4096 (RoCE) | 4096 (IB) |
| Measured, one rail | `ib_write_bw` 392 Gb/s = 98% of line rate | nccl sendrecv 48.8 GB/s = 98% of one rail |

**Same on paper:** line rate per NIC and per node, NICs per GPU, PCIe Gen5. **Different:** Ethernet (RoCEv2) vs InfiniBand, and only the B200 network can reduce data inside the switch (SHARP). The UEC features of Pollara are not used in these runs.

**Apple-to-apple: no.** Both are 7B dense models in BF16, data-parallel (TP = PP = 1), no recompute, and both report Megatron's own TFLOP/s/GPU, but:

| | MI355X | B200 |
|---|---|---|
| Model | llama2-7B: 32 layers, hidden 4096, FFN 11008 | GPT-7B: 36 layers, hidden 4096, FFN 14336 |
| Sequence length | 4096 | 2048 |
| Micro / global batch | 4 / 32 × N (8 steps of accumulation) | 4 / 128 × N (32 steps) |
| Software | Primus v26.5 (AMD-tuned Megatron with fused kernels), distributed optimizer | NGC Megatron-LM |
| Iterations | 50 | 100 |

The longer sequence on MI355X gives more work per step, which tends to raise TFLOP/s. So the 1.12–1.19x is a mix of hardware and software tuning.

**Apple-to-apple: partly.**
- Same: model and weights, input/output lengths, `max-num-seqs` (table 6.1), KV-cache type, prefix caching off, median TPOT.
- Different and unavoidable: **8 GPUs vs 16 GPUs**. B200 needs two nodes and pipeline parallelism, which adds a network hop per token and blocks speculative decoding.
- Different by choice: engine (ATOM / vLLM recipe on MI355X, vLLM on B200), software versions.
- Reading: **one user's answer streams ≈1.4–1.9x faster on B200** (lower TPOT). **Per GPU, MI355X serves 1.05–2.4x more tokens**, and with 256 users or more one MI355X node beats two B200 nodes in total.
